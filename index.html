Narrative Writing

Narrative Writing is a writing style that allows the writer to tell a story or recount an event.

The use of characters, setting, plot, conflict, and resolution in a narrative text/story is very important. Try to write the story in chronological order for a clearer understanding (OPTIONAL). Use dialogue and emotions to the best of your abilities. Using impact verbs is important; they are used to evoke a sense of action in the reader. Emotive language on the other hand, is used to evoke emotion in a reader. Use sensory and figurative language.

There are 6 parts to a narrative text
⦁ Exposition - Introduces the reader to characters, setting, and story (USED TO HOOK THE READER)
⦁ Conflict - Is the main problem faced by the characters
⦁ Rising Action - Part of the story where tension and suspense rise
⦁ Climax - Is the PEAK of the story, the most intense or emotional part of the story
⦁ Falling Action - Tension and suspense calm down, events after the climax
⦁ Resolution - The story's ending/conclusion, conflict is solved (In most cases) (GIVES READER CLOSURE)

Script as a Narrative

RINGGGGG

The cries of the telephone rang through the quiet hallway. Right beside the phone sat a tall, red-headed man with broad shoulders and bright, restless eyes.

"Hello, who is this?" said the man in a lively voice.

"DAVID, we need you here at the station ASAP! We finally got a case!"

David jolted upright, the words still ringing through his head, his heart beating out of his chest as he scrambled for his brand new clothes. He took his coat and sprinted out of his apartment towards the police station.

The city looked as it always did, breathtaking. The cobblestone street was lined with multiple modest houses. The yelling of vendors usually annoyed David, but today they were music to his ears.

He finally reached the station. BANG. He burst through the door.

A WEEPING, wrinkly old granny greeted David's eyes. To the granny's left stood James Maddison, a short, bald, rat-like man who was a journalist in the day and a detective at night. At the corner of the room stood Marcus Bartty, a well-built, blond man.

Marcus quickly ran through the case with David. The mayor's 7-year-old daughter had disappeared earlier this morning, and the granny in front of them was the last person to see her. The granny looked about fifty, with silver hair pulled neatly into a bun. Marcus and David questioned the old lady while James jotted down key information in his notepad.

The granny was Jane's (the mayor's daughter) caretaker, and she had seen Jane frolicking around in the garden shortly before she disappeared.

"Oh dear, I forgot to mention that I saw a rather short man by the fences before I left Jane alone to prepare breakfast," said the granny.

Marcus pressed her for information about the man, but it came to no avail. David asked her if the three of them could scout the area to find clues that would lead them to answers.

"Of course, dear, all I want is that we find Jane as soon as possible," said the granny.

The next day, later in the afternoon, the group headed towards the crime scene. They strutted along the street. The house stood out like white in a pool of black.

The house was perfect; it wasn't too big nor was it too small. The architectural integrity of the house was unlike anything ever seen in history, but the garden was a humble size; it had small white fences surrounding it.

"Are you guys here for the girl?" said a hoarse voice.

A middle-aged man appeared behind them. He quickly explained that he was Jane's neighbor and that he saw a bald man standing by the bushes this morning. Unexpectedly, James shooed him off, and the group made their way to the front door.

Marcus asked for permission from the mayor to scout the premises.

It had been hours, yet none of them found anything that could lead them towards the perpetrator, until... David had spotted a gold, scratched pocket watch by the bushes. He held the watch. It felt cold. He turned the watch over. It had two words engraved in fancy, bold letters:

"James Maddison"

Descriptive Writing

Descriptive writing is a writing style that enables the writer to create a vivid picture in the reader's mind using words.

When writing a descriptive text, remember "SHOW, DON'T TELL". Use sensory language (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste) and focus on imagery and detailed adjectives. Try to incorporate figurative language into the text, such as similes and metaphors. The use of figurative and sensory language should be used more in a descriptive text than in a narrative.

There are 3 parts to a descriptive text
⦁ WIDE ANGLE SHOT (OPENING/INTRODUCTION) - Sets the scene
⦁ ZOOM IN (BODY) - Goes into detail on specific elements/objects
⦁ WIDE ANGLE SHOT IN A DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVE (ENDING/CONCLUSION) - Pulls back, gives reflection and closure
